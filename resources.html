Insurance Glossary

Key terms explained in plain English.

Deductible

The amount you pay out of pocket before your insurance coverage begins to pay. For example, if you have a $500 deductible and file a $3,000 claim, you pay $500 and your insurer pays $2,500.

Learn More →

Premium

The amount you pay regularly (monthly, quarterly, or annually) to maintain your insurance coverage. Your premium is based on factors like risk, coverage amount, and claims history.

Learn More →

Liability Coverage

Coverage that protects you if you're found legally responsible for causing injury or damage to someone else. It typically includes bodily injury and property damage liability.

Learn More →

Rider / Endorsement

An add-on to your policy that modifies or expands your coverage. For example, a rider might add coverage for a specific valuable item not included in your standard policy.

Learn More →

Exclusion

Specific events or circumstances that your policy does not cover. Understanding exclusions is critical — standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood and earthquake damage.

Learn More →

Umbrella Policy

Additional liability coverage that goes beyond the limits of your home, auto, or other underlying policies. It provides an extra layer of financial protection.

Learn More →

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from our clients.

When should I review my insurance coverage?

You should review your coverage at least annually, and whenever you experience a major life change such as getting married, having a child, buying a home, starting a business, or changing jobs.

Can I bundle my insurance policies?

Many insurers offer discounts for bundling multiple policies, such as combining auto and homeowners insurance. Ask your DFIS agent about bundling options available to you.

How much coverage do I need?

The right amount of coverage depends on your individual circumstances, assets, and risk tolerance. That's exactly what Coverage Compass helps you explore. For personalized advice, speak with a DFIS agent.

What's the difference between actual cash value and replacement cost?

Actual cash value considers depreciation, so you'd receive the current value of an item. Replacement cost covers the amount to replace the item at today's prices without deducting for depreciation.
